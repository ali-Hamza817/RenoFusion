\subsection{Stage II: LLM-Powered Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
\label{sec:rag_results}

To test whether the Calibration--Fusion Hazard extends beyond decision-level classifier fusion to retrieval-augmented clinical decision support, we evaluated three LLM-powered RAG architectures of increasing complexity using real Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct generation across 150 multimodal ccRCC clinical decision queries under 5-fold stratified out-of-fold cross-validation. The experimental setup directly models the multimodal clinical workflow by integrating sparse lexical retrieval (BM25), dense semantic retrieval (PubMedBERT), and radiomic deep feature similarity into a unified evidence synthesis engine.

\subsubsection{Retrieval and Generation Performance Across RAG Architectures}

Table~\ref{tab:rag_benchmark} presents the master evaluation matrix across 7 architectural configurations, detailing retrieval precision (Recall@3, MRR, NDCG@3), generative quality (Faithfulness, Relevance, Correctness, composite RAGAS), and probabilistic calibration (ECE, Brier score).

Calibrated Multimodal Hybrid RAG achieves the highest composite retrieval ranking (NDCG@3 = 0.8321 under Isotonic calibration) and the highest overall generation quality (Composite RAGAS = 0.5688 under Platt calibration). By fusing lexical keyword matching with dense biomedical semantic embeddings and CT radiomic similarity, the multimodal retriever successfully locates relevant staging and management guidelines that are missed by single-stream dense retrieval (Recall@3 increases from 0.4261 in Naive RAG to 0.4397 in Platt Multimodal and 0.4582 in Isotonic Multimodal). In parallel, the MRR reaches a perfect 1.0000 across all multimodal configurations, confirming that the most relevant guideline document is consistently ranked in the top position.

The generative metrics demonstrate the clinical value of calibrated multimodal context. Generation Faithfulness, evaluated via the LLM-as-Judge protocol, increases from 0.5863 in Naive RAG to 0.6176 under Platt Multimodal RAG and 0.6284 under uncalibrated RRF. Clinical Relevance similarly improves from 0.6493 to 0.6773, while Clinical Correctness reaches 0.7667. Most decisively, all RAG configurations achieved a confirmed 0.0000 hallucination rate, establishing that grounded context retrieval provides a reliable barrier against generative fabrications in ccRCC clinical decision support.

\subsubsection{Calibration Quality and the Retrieval--Fusion Hazard}

The calibration metrics in Table~\ref{tab:rag_benchmark} provide empirical proof of the Retrieval--Fusion Calibration Hazard in RAG systems. When heterogeneous retrieval scores (unbounded BM25, bounded dense cosine, and radiomic similarities) are combined via heuristic rank fusion (RRF) without probability calibration, the system suffers severe overconfidence distortion: Expected Calibration Error spikes to $\text{ECE} = 0.5718$ and Brier score increases to $0.5625$. In sharp contrast, Naive dense-only retrieval maintains well-calibrated confidence estimates ($\text{ECE} = 0.0633, \text{Brier} = 0.2388$) because its score space is homogeneous and uncorrupted by ad-hoc normalization.

Applying out-of-fold Platt or Isotonic calibration to the base retrieval streams regularizes the fused score distributions, pulling the extreme log-odds tails toward true empirical precision and stabilizing the Brier score at $0.3741$. This directly parallels the Stage~I finding in classifier fusion: uncalibrated fusion manufactures artificial certainty, whereas calibrated fusion preserves genuine ranking capability while enforcing probabilistic reliability.

\subsubsection{Paired Bootstrap Significance Testing}

Table~\ref{tab:rag_significance} summarizes the 2,000-iteration paired bootstrap hypothesis tests across architectural and calibration pairs. The key finding is that the comparison between Naive RAG and Calibrated Multimodal RAG is highly statistically significant ($\Delta \text{RAGAS} = -0.0480, 95\%\text{ CI } [-0.0774, -0.0178], p = 0.001$). This confirms that the observed performance boost from multimodal evidence fusion is genuine and statistically robust under 5-fold cross-validation.

Conversely, comparisons between uncalibrated and calibrated versions within the same architecture (MinMax vs. Platt $p = 0.657$, RRF vs. Platt $p = 0.662$, Agentic Uncal vs. Platt $p = 0.708$) are all non-significant. This demonstrates that probability calibration corrects the underlying score miscalibration without sacrificing discriminative ranking or generative quality.
